Indian weddings are rarely just one event. From the mehendi and haldi to the sangeet and wedding ceremony, each occasion has its own energy—and your outfit can change with it.

Whether you're dressing up for a friend's wedding or a family celebration, the key is to look festive without trying to outshine the couple.

**What Should Women Wear to an Indian Wedding?**

For the wedding ceremony or reception, a saree, lehenga, anarkali or elegant suit are classic choices. For a more contemporary look, try a pre-draped saree, embellished co-ord or Indo-western outfit.

For a mehendi or daytime function, lighter fabrics and brighter colours work well. For the sangeet or reception, you can experiment with richer colours, metallic details and statement accessories.

Don't feel like you need to buy something completely new. Look through your closet first. A saree you've worn before can feel completely different with a new blouse, jewellery or styling.

When you build your wardrobe, prioritise versatile pieces that can work across multiple occasions rather than outfits you'll wear once.

**What Should Men Wear to an Indian Wedding?**

For men, a kurta-pyjama, kurta with trousers, bandhgala or sherwani can work depending on how formal the event is.

A lighter kurta works well for daytime events, while a structured bandhgala or embroidered sherwani can suit an evening wedding.

You can also build your own wardrobe around versatile ethnic pieces—a well-fitted kurta, neutral trousers, a classic Nehru jacket and a pair of traditional shoes can create several different looks.

**How to Choose the Right Wedding Outfit**

Before heading to a wardrobe shop, consider the event, venue, time of day and dress code.

Your personal wardrobe style matters too. If you prefer minimal dressing, you don't need heavy embroidery just because it's a wedding. If you enjoy statement fashion, experiment with colour, texture and accessories.

A good wardrobe stylist would consider your existing clothes, proportions and preferences—not simply suggest whatever is trending.

The same principle applies when you make a wardrobe or build a closet: choose pieces that work with your lifestyle and with each other.

Ultimately, a stylist wardrobe isn't about owning the most clothes. It's about knowing how to put the right ones together.
